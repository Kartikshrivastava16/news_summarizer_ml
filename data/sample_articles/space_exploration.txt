NASA's Artemis program successfully landed two astronauts on the lunar south pole, marking
the first crewed Moon landing in over 50 years. The mission focused on the Shackleton Crater
region, where water ice deposits were confirmed by earlier robotic missions. Astronauts spent
72 hours on the surface conducting geological surveys and deploying scientific instruments.
The mission also tested a new life-support backpack designed for long-duration lunar stays.
NASA administrator said this is a stepping stone toward establishing a permanent lunar base
by 2035. International partners including ESA and JAXA contributed hardware to the mission.
The success has reignited public interest in space exploration, with live viewership breaking
streaming records worldwide.
