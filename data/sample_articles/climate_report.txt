A new United Nations report warns that global average temperatures could rise by 2.5 degrees
Celsius above pre-industrial levels by 2050 if current emission trends continue. The report,
compiled by over 800 scientists across 60 countries, highlights that the last decade was the
warmest on record. Sea levels have risen by 20 cm since 1900, and the pace is accelerating.
Governments are being urged to triple renewable energy investments and phase out coal by 2030.
The report also notes positive developments: solar energy costs have dropped 89% in the past
decade, and electric vehicle adoption has surpassed earlier projections. However, deforestation
in tropical regions continues to undermine carbon-capture efforts at an alarming rate.
